citeval — worked examples

citeval checks whether the paper cited to support a scientific claim actually supports it — catching retraction, species/population scope mismatch, and graded substantive over-reach. Every verdict carries a confidence tier and a provenance label.

These four cases are drawn verbatim from the 30-case calibration corpus and rendered from cached results — no API key is needed to view this page. Objective axes (retraction, scope) are read from structured records and are authoritative; the substantive-support grade is model-inferred by an adversarial supporter/refuter/critic panel with a self-consistency confidence.

metadata-grounded read from a structured record (Retraction Watch, MeSH tags + tree numbers, NCBI linkage). Authoritative.
text-grounded an explicit lexical marker in the claim wording.
model-inferred a model judgement — the C3 claim-scope hint plus the graded substantive-support axis, produced by the adversarial supporter/refuter/critic agent with calibration.
stub-not-adjudicated deliberately not decided in the thin slice.

Metadata (grounded) always overrides model/text on objective axes — a conflict is scored as the model being wrong, not the record.

A25

A finding — and an author — the cited paper never contained

substantive support

The sentence credits “Chen Guanghui” with showing Mfn2 inhibits VSMC proliferation independent of mitochondrial fusion. The cited paper is about miR-214 and fibroblast differentiation in rats — it contains neither that finding nor that author. Graded unsupported-for-the-claim at high self-consistency (and the paper is also retracted).

Claim (verbatim)

Chen Guanghui found that Mfn2 can significantly inhibit VSMC proliferation in vitro and in vivo, and this action has nothing to do with mitochondrial fusion; Furthermore rats and humans have 95.2% Mfn2 homology 35.

— as cited in PMC6854371

Cited paper

MicroRNA-214 Affects Fibroblast Differentiation of Adipose-Derived Mesenchymal Stem Cells by Targeting Mitofusin-2 during Pelvic Floor Dysfunction in SD Rats with Birth Trauma.
Cellular physiology and biochemistry : international journal of experimental cellular physiology, biochemistry, and pharmacology · PMID 28772265 · DOI 10.1159/000479570

A02

The science checks out — but the paper was retracted

retraction

The GAS5 / miR-21 / SOX5 sorafenib-sensitivity claim is substantively supported by the cited paper's own results (graded fully-supported). But Retraction Watch records the paper as retracted for data/image duplication and paper-mill concerns — so the citation cannot stand. Metadata overrides the model: overall NOT SUPPORTED.

Claim (verbatim)

In terms of its impact on sorafenib resistance, it was shown to act as a sponge for miR-21 and upregulation of GAS5 resulted in likewise upregulation of the transcription factor sex determining region Y-box protein 5 (SOX5), conferring increased sensitivity to sorafenib [19].

— as cited in PMC7463785

Cited paper

Long non-coding RNA GAS5 sensitizes renal cell carcinoma to sorafenib via miR-21/SOX5 pathway.
Cell cycle (Georgetown, Tex.) · PMID 29895198 · DOI 10.1080/15384101.2018.1475826

Overall: NOT SUPPORTED AS STATED (CITATION RELIES ON A RETRACTED PAPER) — substantive grade: fully-supported (held separately)

Bs05

Right substance, wrong species

species scope

The paper fully supports that bacteriophages modulate the gut microbiota and the host — substance held constant, graded fully-supported. But it is a mouse study (MeSH: Animals, Mice; no “Humans”), while the claim asserts relevance to human health. That species-scope mismatch is carried on its own axis, so it never silently depresses the substantive grade.

Claim (verbatim)

While much focus is on the bacterial component of this community, recent studies highlight the importance of other microorganisms such as bacteriophages (125, 126) and fungi (127, 128) in human health.

— as cited in PMC7596417

Cited paper

Dynamic Modulation of the Gut Microbiota and Metabolome by Bacteriophages in a Mouse Model.

A clean, high-confidence match

substantive support
