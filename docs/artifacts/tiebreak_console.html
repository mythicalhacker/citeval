The blind tiebreak console — 4/6, with the interpretive calls and the losses on the table

Six times, citeval’s grade disagreed with an external reference. A third, blind reader broke each tie. citeval came out ahead 4–2 — but two of those wins need an interpretive call, and two cases are honest losses. Here is exactly how the number is built, so you can lower it to 2/6 yourself if you don’t buy the calls.

The rule. On the six-level ordinal the blind read “sides with” whichever verdict it lands closer to. An off-scale N/A — non-substantiating read means the paper doesn’t back this claim, so it sides with the more skeptical (higher) of the two grades. Strict counts only exact agreement with citeval; Full adds nearest-ordinal and skeptical-N/A calls.
